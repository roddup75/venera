\section{Baseline inputs}

Let $A_0$ be current AUM, $s=A/A_0$, $q_i$ the total positive parent-order notional, $V_i$ its average daily traded value and $x_i=q_i/V_i$. Orders and ADV must use the same currency. Buys and sells enter as positive traded amounts. Each order scales proportionally with AUM; liquidity, portfolio construction, order frequency and turnover stay fixed.

A trade CSV supplies \texttt{q} and \texttt{adv}, with weights $w_i=q_i/\sum_j q_j$. With bucket inputs, each bucket supplies one editable representative $x_b$ (\texttt{p}, a \textbf{fraction}, despite its legacy name), traded value $Q_b$ and count $n_b$. We use $w_b=Q_b/\sum_j Q_j$; reported rounded share percentages do not determine weights. Counts determine bucket-membership probabilities in Monte Carlo and the count-weighted multi-day diagnostic; traded values determine the notional of each proxy order. Existing buckets describe total parent orders, as confirmed by the user.

The bucket version approximates the unknown within-bucket distribution. Generally $g(E[D])\ne E[g(D)]$, so representative points do not reproduce a full trade tape. Vary representative points within their bounds to assess this assumption, especially for large orders. For an open-ended tail, supply an explicit finite representative and stress range. No universe ADV distribution is needed once order/ADV ratios and notional weights are available.

\section{Execution time and absolute signal capture}

With a fixed daily participation fraction $p$ and signal half-life $h$ in trading days:

\begin{equation}
D_i(s)=\frac{s x_i}{p},\qquad \lambda=\frac{\log 2}{h}.
\end{equation}

Uniform execution over $[0,D]$ gives:

\begin{equation}
g(D;h)=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda D}}{\lambda D},\qquad g(0;h)=1.
\end{equation}

Execution times are continuous trading-day equivalents: an order can finish intraday. There is no minimum one-day floor, horizon cap, grandfathered participation or unfinished-order adjustment. This model assumes every order eventually completes; it does not simulate overlapping orders, cancellation or changing signals.

\section{Relative alpha capture}

\begin{equation}
G(s)=\sum_i w_i g(D_i(s);h),\qquad H(s)=\frac{G(s)}{G(1)}.
\end{equation}

The dashboard reports \textbf{relative alpha capture} as $100H(s)$ and \textbf{absolute capture} as $100G(s)$. Relative capture equals 100\% at current AUM. In Monte Carlo, apply this normalization within each book and average its results. This is an economic multiplier, not a measured portfolio-construction transfer coefficient. Normalize the weighted aggregate once; do not average per-order capture ratios.

\section{Fundamental Law of Active Management}

The extended Fundamental Law links expected gross information ratio to forecast skill, independent breadth and portfolio-construction efficiency:

\begin{equation}
IR_{\rm gross}\approx IC\sqrt{BR}\,TC_{\rm construction}.
\end{equation}

Here $IC$ is the information coefficient, $BR$ is the number of independent investment decisions per year and $TC_{\rm construction}$ is the construction transfer coefficient. Parent-order count and number of holdings are not estimates of independent breadth. See \href{https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/analysis-active-portfolio-management}{CFA Institute, Analysis of Active Portfolio Management}.

In this implementation, observed baseline gross alpha after baseline execution anchors the combined baseline effect:

\begin{equation}
\frac{\alpha_{g,0}}{\sigma_{TE}}\approx IC\sqrt{BR}\,TC_{\rm eff}(1),\qquad TC_{\rm eff}(s)=TC_{\rm eff}(1)H(s).
\end{equation}

This last relation is a reduced-form economic mapping. Effective TC incorporates implementation capture; neither it nor $H$ is asserted to be a measured forecast/position correlation. Holding forecast skill, independent breadth and construction efficiency fixed as AUM scales gives:

\begin{equation}
IR_{\rm net}(s)=\frac{\alpha_{g,0}}{\sigma_{TE}}H(s)-\frac{C_0s^\gamma}{100\sigma_{TE}}.
\end{equation}

The model does not estimate $IC$, $BR$ or construction TC separately from liquidity buckets. It does not multiply observed gross alpha by these factors again. The execution-capture assumption is an additional model choice, not a result implied by the Fundamental Law alone.

\section{Net alpha, annual costs and IR}

Baseline gross alpha $\alpha_{g,0}$ is annual alpha \textbf{after baseline execution delay and before the modeled annual costs}. An ideal frictionless backtest alpha cannot be used interchangeably without adjusting the baseline.

\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\rm net}(s)=\alpha_{g,0}H(s)-\frac{C_0s^\gamma}{100},\qquad IR_{\rm net}(s)=\frac{\alpha_{\rm net}(s)}{\sigma_{TE}}.
\end{equation}

Alpha and tracking error above are in percentage points; $C_0$ is total annual portfolio cost in basis points. At $s=1$, net alpha is baseline gross alpha minus $C_0/100$. At the default $\gamma=0$, annual cost \textbf{in portfolio bp} stays constant; dollar costs grow with AUM. This is a simplifying assumption, not an empirical impact law. Positive $\gamma$ is an optional cost stress, not a fitted impact exponent. Participation sensitivities hold the selected cost rule fixed and do not claim to estimate the cost of changing execution speed.

Legacy saved strategies receive an initial annual-cost input from:

\begin{equation}
C_0=2\left(\frac{T_{\rm one\ way,\%}}{100}\right)\frac{\sum_b Q_bc_b}{\sum_b Q_b}.
\end{equation}

Here $c_b$ is cost in bp per traded dollar and one-way turnover is defined so that buys plus sells / AUM is twice one-way turnover. This conversion avoids ambiguity about the two traded sides. After conversion, annual cost is an explicit input; changing bucket data or trade CSVs does not automatically recalibrate it. Include each modeled cost once. Tracking error remains fixed.

\section{Monte Carlo simulation}

Monte Carlo is the default calculation mode, with \textbf{2,000 samples} and \textbf{random seed 2026}. Both are editable sidebar parameters and are saved with the strategy. Samples mean independently resampled books, not individual trades. Deterministic mode remains an optional reference.

For an actual trade CSV with $N$ rows, sample $N$ complete $(q_i,V_i)$ pairs with replacement, uniformly by order. Preserve each sampled order's notional and order/ADV ratio, then normalize its notional weight. This preserves the observed relationship between size and liquidity within each pair.

For aggregate buckets, use $N=\operatorname{round}(\sum_b n_b)$ proxy orders per book and sample bucket membership with probabilities $P_b=n_b/\sum_j n_j$. A proxy order in bucket $b$ has notional $\bar q_b=Q_b/n_b$ and the editable representative ratio $x_b$. Thus a book's sampled bucket counts $m_{rb}$ generate notional weights:

\begin{equation}
 (m_{r1},\ldots,m_{rB})\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N;P_1,\ldots,P_B),\qquad w_{rb}=\frac{m_{rb}\bar q_b}{\sum_j m_{rj}\bar q_j}.
\end{equation}

A bucket with positive count requires positive traded value, and conversely. Buckets with both zero are permitted. This aggregate bootstrap varies trade composition conditional on representative order sizes; it does not reconstruct the unknown within-bucket size/ADV distribution or draw fresh universe ADV values. Individual books need not exactly match observed bucket shares or total dollars. Annual cost and baseline gross alpha remain fixed scenario inputs.

For each book $r$, calculate $G_r(s)$, normalize $H_r(s)=G_r(s)/G_r(1)$ and apply the net-alpha and IR equations. Reuse the same sampled book at every AUM checkpoint. Default curves, diagnostics and scenario tables show simulation means. The mean multiplier is:

\begin{equation}
\bar H(s)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^{M}\frac{G_r(s)}{G_r(1)}.
\end{equation}

Average these normalized book results; do not replace this with a ratio of mean captures. Each book has $H_r(1)=1$, so the baseline relative-capture and net-alpha bands collapse by construction. Absolute baseline capture can vary, but baseline-alpha uncertainty is not simulated.

Charts and CSV exports report pointwise 5th and 95th percentiles for relative capture, net alpha and net IR. These are \textbf{conditional trade-resampling ranges}, not full confidence intervals for future alpha or simultaneous curve bands. Forecast uncertainty, half-life/cost parameter uncertainty, within-bucket structure and liquidity-regime changes are excluded. More samples reduce Monte Carlo numerical error; they do not remove those uncertainties or mechanically tighten the underlying resampling distribution. IID order resampling also omits rebalance clustering; timestamped trades would be needed for a block bootstrap.

Capacity thresholds use the mean simulated curve from USD 500m, solved by bisection within the chart horizon. Current AUM remains the order-size and alpha calibration anchor. No probability of capacity breach is inferred from this mean crossing. Sensitivity comparisons use the same seed and book count for each alternative, with each book renormalized to that alternative's baseline. Seed, sample count, mode and percentile columns are included in the capacity export.

\section{Three-point calibration isolating alpha decay}
Calibration triples $(A_i,b_i,I_i)$ contain AUM in USD billions, count-weighted percentage of parent orders taking strictly more than one day (percentage points), and net IR. Defaults are $(0.5,0,0.60)$, $(3,2,0.57)$ and $(6,10,0.50)$. The starting AUM and share are fixed at USD 0.5bn and zero. Subsequent AUM and shares increase strictly; shares lie within 0--100\%. IR decreases strictly and AUM inputs use USD 0.5bn increments.
\begin{align*}
k &= \frac{\log(b_2/b_1)}{\log((A_2-A_0)/(A_1-A_0))}, &
b(A) &= b_1\left(\frac{A-A_0}{A_1-A_0}\right)^k,\\
\eta &= \frac{\log((I_0-I_2)/(I_0-I_1))}{\log(b_2/b_1)}, &
I(b) &= I_0-(I_0-I_1)\left(\frac{b}{b_1}\right)^\eta.
\end{align*}
The defaults imply $k=2.041249$ and $\eta=0.748070$, reproducing all three points exactly. They are user-supplied scenario assumptions, not historical parameter estimates. Multi-day share is a congestion proxy; it does not distinguish two-day and ten-day orders.

Initial net IR includes fixed other effects. Only alpha decay varies; tracking error is fixed. Net alpha in percent equals calibrated net IR times tracking error in percent. Execution half-life, participation, baseline gross alpha, cost drag and cost elasticity do not enter this mode. No additional cost subtraction, decay multiplier or TC multiplier is applied. The empirical backtest comparison remains separate.

Values beyond the last calibration AUM (default USD 6bn) are labelled extrapolation. The model stops when the multi-day percentage reaches 100\%, at
\[
A_{100}=A_0+(A_1-A_0)(100/b_1)^{1/k}.
\]
Charts and scenario tables respect this limit. Capacity crossings use the analytical inverse of the composed central curve, within the active horizon.

In Monte Carlo mode, each AUM uses $K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N,b(A)/100)$, with $N$ equal to the observed parent-order count (CSV rows or rounded aggregate count). Apply $I(100K/N)$ to each draw and report pointwise 5th--95th percentiles for IR and net alpha. Sample count and seed are configurable; defaults remain 2,000 and 2026. The centre curve remains the exact calibration, not the mean of the nonlinear simulated outcomes.

This is synthetic independent-order congestion-count uncertainty, distinct from the ADV-book bootstrap. It excludes uncertainty in the three calibration points and dependence between orders. The ranges are conditional pointwise intervals, not simultaneous confidence bands. They collapse at the zero-congestion anchor. More draws improve numerical precision without removing model uncertainty.

Model selection and triples are saved with a strategy. Strategies without an explicit selection default to the combined empirical model.

\section{Three-point transfer-coefficient calibration}
Input order is $(T_i,A_i,a_i)$: absolute TC in percent, AUM in USD billions and annual net alpha in percent. Other effects remain fixed. Default triples are $(60,0.5,1.0)$, $(40,3,0.6)$ and $(25,6,0.3)$. AUM starts at USD 0.5bn and increases in USD 0.5bn increments; TC decreases strictly within 0--100\% and alpha decreases strictly.
\begin{align*}
k_T &= \frac{\log((T_0-T_2)/(T_0-T_1))}{\log((A_2-A_0)/(A_1-A_0))},\\
T(A) &= T_0-(T_0-T_1)\left(\frac{A-A_0}{A_1-A_0}\right)^{k_T},\\
\eta_T &= \frac{\log((a_0-a_2)/(a_0-a_1))}{\log((T_0-T_2)/(T_0-T_1))},\\
a(T) &= a_0-(a_0-a_1)\left(\frac{T_0-T}{T_0-T_1}\right)^{\eta_T}.
\end{align*}
These curves reproduce all three estimated triples exactly. Defaults imply nonlinear AUM-to-TC decay but $\eta_T=1$, a linear TC-to-alpha link:
\[
a(T)=0.02T-0.20.
\]
Here TC and annual alpha use percentage-point units. The fixed offset comes from the supplied net-alpha estimates; this is not a pure proportional FLAM gross-alpha curve through zero. It does not estimate IC or independent breadth. Editing the triples can produce a nonlinear TC-to-alpha relationship.

Net IR equals annual net alpha divided by fixed tracking error, using percent units for both return quantities. No extra costs, execution capture or separate alpha-decay multiplier are applied. Projections beyond the final calibration AUM (default USD 6bn) are labelled extrapolation and stop at zero TC:
\[
A_{T=0}=A_0+(A_1-A_0)\left(\frac{T_0}{T_0-T_1}\right)^{1/k_T}.
\]
Capacity crossings use the analytical inverse of the central curve, within the active chart horizon. TC calibration is deterministic: three estimated triples identify no TC uncertainty distribution, so no simulation band is inferred. Execution and alpha-decay models retain their separate Monte Carlo options. Model selection, triples and fitted curvatures are exported; strategy saving persists the selection and triples.

Observed aggregate impact uses actual traded-value weights, not trade-count weights or rounded shares. Default estimated impact is about 8.6932 bp per traded dollar. \section{Combined three-pillar empirical IR curve}
The selected combination multiplies retained-alpha factors. Let $I_\alpha(A)$ be the alpha-decay calibration and $I_0=I_\alpha(0.5)$ its initial net IR. Let $a_{TC}(A)$ be the TC calibration's annual alpha, with $a_{TC,0}=a_{TC}(0.5)$. Define
\begin{align*}
H_\alpha(A)&=\frac{I_\alpha(A)}{I_0},&H_{TC}(A)&=\frac{a_{TC}(A)}{a_{TC,0}},\\
C(A)&=2\tau\bar c(A),&IR_{\mathrm{emp}}(A)&=I_0H_\alpha(A)H_{TC}(A)-\frac{C(A)-C(0.5)}{100\sigma_{TE}}.
\end{align*}
Here $C$ is annual portfolio impact drag in bp, $\tau$ is one-way annual turnover as a fraction and $\sigma_{TE}$ is tracking error in percentage points. Net alpha in percent is $IR_{\mathrm{emp}}\sigma_{TE}$. The initial net IR includes baseline costs, so only the incremental impact cost above USD 0.5bn is deducted. The TC alpha calibration supplies a relative factor, not a second absolute alpha anchor. Legacy gross alpha, half-life, cost elasticity and explicit annual-cost controls do not enter this calculation.

With default triples, IR before incremental impact costs is $0.57\times0.60=0.342$ at USD 3bn and $0.50\times0.30=0.150$ at USD 6bn. At USD 0.5bn net IR is exactly 0.60. Current AUM anchors observed order sizes used for impact estimation; the net-IR anchor remains USD 0.5bn.

The three deductions are alpha-decay loss, TC loss and incremental impact loss, respectively.

\section{What Monte Carlo samples and the alpha assumption}
Monte Carlo samples are alternative trading books and congestion outcomes. The implementation does not draw an individual alpha forecast for each trade, or assume a normal distribution of trade alpha. The user-supplied alpha-decay curve converts the simulated multi-day trade percentage into portfolio IR.

\textbf{Alpha sensitivity.} The decay calibration treats parent orders as exchangeable for congestion purposes: a small order and a large order count equally toward the percentage taking more than one day. It does not identify which orders carry more predictive alpha or have greater delay sensitivity. This is a count-based approximation that ignores trade-level alpha heterogeneity, rather than an explicit allocation of equal alpha dollars to every order. Impact costs are aggregated separately using traded-notional weights.

\textbf{Bucket-input sampling.} A simulated book has $N=\operatorname{round}(\sum_b n_b)$ orders. Its bucket counts follow
\[
(m_1,\ldots,m_B)\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N;P_1,\ldots,P_B),
\qquad P_b=\frac{n_b}{\sum_j n_j}.
\]
Each proxy order uses its bucket's representative order/ADV and average notional $Q_b/n_b$. Randomness changes the counts and composition; the current sampler does not draw a continuous order/ADV value within each bucket.

\textbf{Actual-trade sampling.} With a trade CSV, the empirical bootstrap draws $N$ observed orders uniformly with replacement, retaining each sampled order's complete $(q,ADV)$ pair. Equal selection probability does not establish equal alpha contribution.

\textbf{Congestion sampling.} Independently of the cost-book draw, at each AUM the combined model generates
\[
K(A)\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(N,p(A)),\qquad
p(A)=\frac{\text{calibrated multi-day percentage at }A}{100}.
\]
The sampled percentage $100K(A)/N$ enters the calibrated alpha-to-IR function. TC retention and all calibration parameters remain fixed. The binomial assumption uses independent order indicators with the same conditional probability $p(A)$.

The model therefore omits dependence between sampled impact-cost composition and alpha loss: difficult-to-execute trades may carry unusually high or low alpha in reality. The conditional percentile bands do not capture this dependence or uncertainty in the calibration assumptions. Increasing the sample count improves numerical precision without adding trades to each book or resolving these omitted sources of uncertainty. The seed makes the draws reproducible.
